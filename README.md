# Lesguardians

> **An independent game-development community and programming forum
> project --- developed between 2017 and 2018.**

![Lesguardians](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/107876267/174682174-65f67d09-88f2-4c89-a54f-fa83581ad92f.png)

## Overview

**Lesguardians** was a Brazilian community project created in 2017 with
the goal of building an accessible space for programmers, artists, and
independent game developers to exchange knowledge, discuss technology,
and collaborate around game development.

The project was built around **Invision Power Board (IP.Board)** and
expanded through front-end customization, web technologies, multilingual
resources, community organization, original visual design, and content
production.

I was responsible for the project's management, community leadership,
technical maintenance, content organization, front-end work, and part of
its visual identity. Lesguardians was eventually paused and discontinued
in 2018, primarily because maintaining its technical, editorial, and
community responsibilities as a one-person project became financially
and operationally unsustainable.

Today, I preserve this repository as a record of an early project that
helped me develop practical experience in web development, project
management, community building, UI/UX decisions, content strategy, and
the realities of maintaining a growing online platform.

## Project Characteristics

Lesguardians was designed as more than a general discussion forum. Its
structure was centered on programming, game development, independent
production, and the different disciplines involved in creating games.

The project included:

-   a customized **Invision Power Board** installation;
-   an original responsive visual theme;
-   native/basic support for **Brazilian Portuguese, English, French,
    and Spanish**;
-   additional multilingual accessibility through integration with
    Google Translate;
-   multiple discussion areas dedicated to programming and the game
    industry;
-   community spaces intended for developers, artists, musicians,
    composers, voice actors, streamers, and players;
-   a simple manually managed advertising/monetization model;
-   custom visual assets and mascots created specifically for the
    project.

## Goals

The central idea behind Lesguardians was to reduce some of the barriers
faced by people entering independent game development.

The project aimed to make technical information easier to discover and
discuss, encourage collaboration between people with different creative
and technical skills, and provide a place where beginners and more
experienced developers could exchange knowledge.

Its broader objectives included supporting independent and open-source
development, expanding accessible game-development content, connecting
different creative disciplines, and experimenting with community and
business models that could support an independent technology platform.

## My Role

Lesguardians was largely a one-person project.

My responsibilities included:

**Project & Community Management**\
Planning the platform, organizing its areas, defining community
objectives, managing users and maintaining the overall direction of the
project.

**Web Development & Maintenance**\
Customizing the CMS, working on the front-end presentation, maintaining
the website, integrating features and addressing technical issues.

**Content Production**\
Writing and organizing articles and educational material for multiple
areas of the forum.

**UI/UX & Information Architecture**\
Designing the website structure, navigation, categories, layout and
responsive theme.

**Visual Identity**\
Creating project artwork, mascots and supporting visual assets, with
additional contributions from community collaborators.

This broad scope made Lesguardians an important learning experience, but
it also exposed one of the project's fundamental weaknesses: too many
critical responsibilities depended on a single person.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What Did Not Work --- and What I Learned

### Content production became too complex

The forum covered many different subjects. Maintaining useful content
for all of those areas required substantially more planning, research,
writing and editorial work than initially expected.

### The workload did not scale

Besides technical maintenance and front-end work, I was also responsible
for articles, administration, community management and content
organization. As the scope increased, the workload became difficult to
sustain.

### Project priorities needed stronger separation

Technical, creative, community and business tasks competed for the same
limited amount of time. Features such as monetization received less
attention than they needed because day-to-day maintenance consumed most
of the available effort.

### Navigation became too complex

The large number of categories increased the number of steps required to
reach certain areas. This negatively affected accessibility and
demonstrated the importance of information architecture and reducing
unnecessary interaction.

### Community projects require contributors

Finding people willing to consistently contribute was difficult. A
community platform cannot depend indefinitely on one person for
development, administration, moderation and content production.

### The most important lesson: scalability is also organizational

The project taught me that technical ability alone does not make a large
project sustainable.

A platform can work technically and still fail operationally if
responsibilities, resources and knowledge are concentrated in one
person. Collaboration, delegation, prioritization, financial planning
and a realistic project scope are just as important as implementation.

That lesson became one of the most valuable outcomes of Lesguardians.

## Project Status

**Status:** Archived / discontinued\
**Development period:** 2017--2018\
**Origin:** Brazil

The project is no longer maintained as an active community. This
repository exists as historical documentation of the work, ideas, design
experiments and lessons produced during its development.

## Retrospective

Lesguardians did not become the long-term platform I originally
envisioned, but that does not make the project unsuccessful as a
learning experience.

Building and managing it exposed me to problems that are difficult to
understand only through theory: scope growth, content maintenance, user
experience, technical debt, prioritization, community engagement,
monetization, and the operational cost of maintaining a platform.

Looking back, its most important result was not the website itself, but
the experience gained from designing, building, managing and eventually
evaluating why a complex project could not sustainably remain a
one-person operation.
